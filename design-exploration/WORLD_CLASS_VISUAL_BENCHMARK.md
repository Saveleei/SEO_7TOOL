## Cross-brand patterns

1. **Recognition is systemic.** The strongest brands repeat one product color, typography voice, image behavior, naming architecture, and geometry—not just a logo color.
2. **Scale follows the object.** Large equipment is shown architecturally; small tooling is shown as macro precision. Both avoid timid thumbnail treatment.
3. **Technical data is content, not fine print.** Model codes, diameters, axes, materials, and performance values are visible early and aligned consistently.
4. **Trust is operational.** Repair, engineering support, availability, service life, installed base, training, and applications beat generic “quality” claims.
5. **Navigation reflects work.** Leading brands organize around tasks, processes, systems, and applications in addition to product taxonomies.

## What 7TOOL should learn

- From Hilti: decisive commercial hierarchy and evidence-backed service promises.
- From Festool: system thinking, controlled application photography, and long-term premium restraint.
- From Sandvik, Walter, and Seco: macro detail, technical numerals, and machining-operation logic.
- From Haas, TRUMPF, and DMG MORI: monumental product scale and calm capital-equipment presentation.
- From Bosch and Milwaukee: memorable system naming and consistent family navigation, used with less promotional volume.

## What 7TOOL should not copy

- Hilti red blocks, Bosch blue worlds, Milwaukee's loud uppercase red/black theater, Festool green/blue ecosystem styling, Sandvik/Kennametal yellow, or TRUMPF/DMG corporate visual signatures.
- Manufacturer-specific ownership language: 7TOOL is a specialist supplier across brands and should not imply it manufactures every product.
- Promotion-first homepages, car-like cinematic gloss, dark-theme dominance, oversized lifestyle campaigns, or digital-factory SaaS tropes.
- Technical density without progressive prioritization; a distributor catalog needs expert depth and fast commercial action together.

## Where 7TOOL can be distinctive

1. **The cut as an editorial operation.** The diagonal seven-cut crops one decisive image plane or active edge, never decorates every tile.
2. **Mixed-scale mastery.** One system that makes a 1,100 kg machine and a 40 mm cutter both feel important by switching between product-scale and macro-detail modes.
3. **Russian B2B clarity.** Price with VAT, real stock, delivery, post-payment, quote, and named engineering help presented more calmly than conventional local ecommerce.
4. **Catalog as a technical instrument.** Stable alignment of SKUs, diameters, voltages, weights, and availability can become a recognizable 7TOOL rhythm.
5. **Orange as a calibrated signal.** One focal orange action per viewport makes the color feel proprietary again.
